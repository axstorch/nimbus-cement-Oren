Damage rules

Each rule converts weather into lost production. Edit any of them; everything recalculates.

Output lost per °C of warming2.5%

Peer-reviewed panel of ~70,000 Indian factories: plant output falls about 2–3% per °C, worker productivity 2–4% per °C.

Days lost per heavy-rain day5 d

No study exists. Derived from reported Indian plant shutdowns (3M Ahmedabad, Ambattur, Ludhiana, 2023–26), which ran 2–10 days. Treat as indicative.

Water-curtailment days, groundwater plant8 d/yr

No published frequency data. Judgement default, informed by iFOREST: groundwater's share of cement withdrawals in stressed regions rose 27%→38% in a year, and ~70% of Rajasthan's groundwater units are over-exploited. Surface-water plants get 3 days; desalinated, 0.

Clinker stockpile at grinding units10 d

Not in the source data. A grinding unit keeps running on its clinker pile while its supplier is down, so it only loses the days left over after the pile runs out.

Climate model

Open-Meteo serves one high-emissions pathway only, so these are three models of the same future, not three futures. Disagreement between them is the honest uncertainty range.

Models disagree sharply. Durgapur warms +1.23 °C under MRI and +0.05 °C under MPI. Chittorgarh ranks worst in all three, which is why it is the one finding worth acting on.
Heat shows ₹0 today by design. The heat rule measures loss relative to today's climate, so today's heat cost already sits inside normal operating results.

Concentration risk

Three of the nine plants have no kiln. P07 Mundra takes all its clinker from P01 Chittorgarh; P08 Paradip and P09 Durgapur take theirs from P06 Baloda Bazar.

₹2,597 cr of revenue — 28% of the company — depends on P01 Chittorgarh, a groundwater-fed plant in the Indian state with the highest share of over-exploited groundwater units. Mundra's own water supply is desalinated and scores zero risk, which makes it look safe in isolation. It is not.

Propagation is a tail risk, not a central estimate. At the default 10-day stockpile, nothing propagates: P01 loses about 10 disrupted days a year and the buffer absorbs them. But the model treats those days as one continuous outage. In reality they are scattered — a hot spell in May, a flood day in July — and scattered days are far easier to absorb. Drag the stockpile slider below 10 to see where it starts to bite.

Assumptions

  1. Climate data — Open-Meteo Climate API (CMIP6 HighResMIP downscaled to 10 km), models MRI-AGCM3-2-S, EC-Earth3P-HR, MPI-ESM1-2-XR. Baseline 2015–24, future 2036–45. Source sheet with formulas.
  2. Why a ten-year window. A single year reflects weather, not climate. Both periods are ten-year averages so the comparison isolates the climate signal rather than one year's variability.
  3. Coordinates snapped to grid. The API returns the nearest land cell, up to ~5 km from the plant. Chandrapur was requested at 19.96, 79.30 and returned at 20.00, 79.30.
  4. Heat — output loss = rate × (mean daily-max 2036–45 − mean daily-max 2015–24). Negative warming is floored at zero.
  5. Flood — a heavy-rain day is over 100 mm, between IMD's "heavy" (64.5–115.5 mm) bands. This metric is fragile: MRI sees 0.3–1.7 such days a year, the other two models almost none.
  6. Water — base days by source (groundwater 8, surface 3, desalinated 0), scaled by rainfall change at 2× sensitivity, then by plant type.
  7. Plant type — grinding units carry 25% of a kiln's water risk. A kiln without cooling water stops; a grinding mill largely does not. The 25% is judgement, not a measured figure.
  8. Clinker stockpile: 10 days (editable). Days lost by a grinding unit = supplier's disrupted days − stockpile days, never below zero.
  9. Revenue per day = FY 2025-26 revenue ÷ 365. Output held flat to 2040; no demand growth, no price change, no discounting.
  10. All plants assumed to operate as they do today, with no adaptation investment.

Not in v1

Cyclone and sea level

P07 Mundra and P08 Paradip are coastal: ₹1,130 cr of assets, ₹1,384 cr of revenue. Open-Meteo carries no cyclone data. Would need IBTrACS tracks or ISRO Bhuvan layers.

Transport between plants

Clinker moves P01→P07 by rail and P06→P08/P09. Floods and cyclones cut rail, road and port links as well as plants, so a grinding unit can be stranded while both ends run. Needs network flood data over each corridor.

Transition risk

The carbon half: CCTS intensity targets, carbon price paths, CBAM. Material for cement, but a different question from "climate events".

Asset damage

This model prices lost production, not repair or write-off. A cyclone that destroys a grinding unit is an asset loss and does not appear here.

Percentile flood threshold

A fixed 100 mm cut-off inherits each model's rainfall bias. Using each site's wettest 1% of days would make the metric comparable across models.

Disruption timing

Annual disrupted days are tested against the stockpile as one block. Daily simulation would place them through the year and give a truer propagation figure.
